# Topic: The capitalist class generally remained ambivalent in their support to the Indian National Congress until 1940s. In this context, analyse the varying positions of the Indian capitalists throughout the national movement.

The capitalist class of Tatas and Birlas had a profound impact on the national movement.

**They largely remained ambivalent**

1. **Supported the movement.**
   - During the <u>Swadeshi Movement</u> → enterprises with patriotic zeal, e.g. the Steam Navigation Company of Pillai → Khadi and village atmashakti → support of Tatas → falling foreign imports.
   - During the <u>Non-Cooperation Movement</u> → strikes by workers being supported, especially in British jobs → government jobs and plantations.

However, they only supported when —
1. There was little loss of profits;
2. There were no British pressures;
3. There was patriotic zeal, initially.

**Mostly they withdrew their support, due to the following reasons**

1. Violence and <u>loss of infrastructure</u> — e.g. dug-up roads and railway tracks led to losses.
2. <u>British excesses</u> leading to silence of capitalists.
3. <u>Worker strikes</u> in industry led to loss of labour productivity and indiscipline.
4. Congress was more <u>pro-worker</u> rather than pro-capitalist.
5. <u>Socialism</u> as a model of economy by Nehru ⟹ threatened them.
6. Rise of <u>Communist</u> ideas under M.N. Roy ⟹ threat to capitalism.

*(However, the Industrial Disputes Act of 1938 by the Congress Ministry was pro-capitalist.)*

Yet, they played an important role in —
1. Bringing the <u>capitalist class</u> to India and preventing <u>neo-colonialism</u>.
2. Bringing the <u>Bombay Plan</u> ⟹ finalizing the pattern of growth of India.
3. Building a <u>welfare system</u> via altruism — e.g. Tatas donating to the Tilak fund, 1920.
4. Upholding the capitalist venture of Indian capital for Indian development.

Even today their legacy resonates as we move towards our goal of a <u>developed country by 2047</u> and a <u>$5 trillion economy</u> with jobs for all.
