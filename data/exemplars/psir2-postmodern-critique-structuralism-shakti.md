# Topic: Comment on the post-modernist critique of structuralism.

Structuralism is a school within realism that defines the nature of international politics. It emerged as a criticism of *classical realism* (Morgenthau), which had based its theory on **human nature** — i.e. it was an *actor-level* analysis.

Structuralists like *K. Waltz* and *Mearsheimer* contradicted classical realism for being too subjective, since it rested on human nature. They thus shifted the focus of study of international politics from *actor-level* to *structure-level* analysis — because the structure of international politics is more or less stable, in contrast to human nature. They base the study of IP on the **anarchical structure** and hence argue that states are continuously striving for power maximisation to ensure their security.

*Post-modernists* have criticised structuralism for overlooking the actor-level analysis altogether, and hence suggest that **both the actor and the structure** must be taken into consideration. The reductionism of either level — collapsing politics into human nature, or into structure alone — flattens a reality that is co-constituted by both. Thus the post-modern critique pushes IR toward a more reflexive, agent-and-structure account rather than a single privileged unit of analysis.
