# Topic: Describe the political sociology approach (in comparative politics).

The political sociology approach is a **hybrid concept** in comparative politics which studies the *interface between politics and society* in order to understand the political system better.

This approach is widely used to study the political systems of **post-colonial societies** like India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, etc. — because merely studying their constitution, institutions and state is not sufficient. These are examples of *over-developed states*, where the social context of their societies is more important. Here the focus is on studying the impact of **sociological factors** like caste, religion and ethnicity on the politics of the country. Scholars like *Christophe Jaffrelot* and *Paul Brass* have studied this in the context of India.

Thus the political sociology approach has *broadened the scope* of political science by placing it in the sociological perspective. But it has also been criticised for making political science a **dependent discipline** on sociology. However, we must understand that politics is not independent of society — and so, used carefully, the approach enriches rather than dissolves the discipline.
