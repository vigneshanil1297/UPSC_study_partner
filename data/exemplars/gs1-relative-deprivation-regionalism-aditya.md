The recent <u>Manipur violence</u> and demands for a separate state by certain communities brought regionalism to the fore.

[Causal flow diagram: Threat to culture / livelihood → feeling of relative deprivation → subjective realization of objective reality by a third party → resentment & regionalism.]

Thereby the existence of relative deprivation is crucial for constructing the argument for regionalism.

1. Gives a window for <u>politicization</u> — e.g. demand for <u>Nagalim</u> claiming ethnic assertion and backwardness.
2. Provides the root cause for <u>preserving culture</u> as a demand — e.g. Assam Accord of 1985, and similarly Gorkhaland today.
3. Push for tapping of emotions as linked to <u>language imposition</u> — e.g. <u>Bodoland</u> after Bengali and Assamese language dominance.
4. <u>Perceived</u> relative deprivation due to loss of resources — e.g. the "Mumbai Aamchi" soil/sons-of-the-soil policy.
5. Some other aspects:
   - Better administration due to topography — e.g. Uttarakhand.
   - Development of backward tribals — e.g. Jharkhand.

Thereby we see that relative deprivation, caused either due to cultural or livelihood threat, causes regional demands.
