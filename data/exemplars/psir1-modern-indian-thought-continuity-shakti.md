# Topic: What are the various components of modern Indian political thought? Do you think there is more of a continuity than change?

Modern Indian political thought can be divided into **two phases** — the first half of the 19th century as Phase 1, and the second half as Phase 2.

**Phase 1** is characterised by the rise of *social reform movements*, which took their strongest effervescence in Bengal — hence also called the **"Bengal Renaissance"** — witnessing reformers like *Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, Keshab Chandra Sen, Dayananda Saraswati*. The basic focus of this era was to use *British rule as a modernising force* for the eviction of social evils — hence crusades against *sati*, child marriage, the caste system, and the demand for widow remarriage.

**Phase 2** is characterised by the rise of *nationalist sentiments* and the freedom movement, which carried different meanings for different sections. In its early part the **moderates** considered the British a source of Western knowledge, advocated *Dominion Status*, and resorted to prayer, petition and propaganda. In the later part, nationalism took a *revolutionary and extremist* form, creating new icons like *Bhagat Singh, Jatin Das, Aurobindo Ghosh*. The thought got its much-needed momentum with the entry of **Mahatma Gandhi**, who propagated *Ahimsa* and *Truth* and the method of *Satyagraha*.

Though we see an *evolution* of thought throughout the period, the **end goal has always remained the same** — the freedom of mother India, not just from *external* British rule but also from *internal evils* like the subjugation of women and the caste system. As **V.R. Mehta** notes, unlike the West, India does not value things in dichotomies; we observe **continuity** instead. Hence there is more continuity than change.
