Evolution of the press in India began with the <u>Bengal Gazette</u> of James Augustus Hickey in <u>1785</u>.

**Evolution of press** [timeline diagram of milestones:]
- First press by the French & Portuguese (17th century) →
- Masses get access due to the Bengal Gazette →
- Licensing regulation of 1823 →
- Metcalfe as "liberator" of the press, 1835 →
- Vernacular Press Act, 1878 →
- Repeal of VPA by Ripon, 1882 →
- Wartime restrictions of 1910–14.

This had an instrumental impact on various stages of the Indian freedom struggle.

**INITIAL**
1. Developed <u>anti-imperial sentiment</u> — e.g. via *Voice of India*, Dadabhai Naoroji.
2. Helped bring political education to the masses — e.g. the library movement of the 19th century.

**GANDHI PHASE**
3. Helped build the people to act — e.g. *Commonweal* of Annie Besant popularized mass movement.
4. Mass mobilization via <u>pamphlets</u> — e.g. during the Non-Cooperation Movement.
5. <u>Criticism of government</u> — e.g. Jogendra Chandra Bose in *Bangobasi* → Age of Consent Act.

**However, they were faced by stiff repression by the British**
1. 1823 regulations made it criminal to operate a press without a licence.
2. 1878 <u>Vernacular Press Act</u>, called the "Gagging Act" by moderates → seize press equipment.
3. <u>Sedition law</u> brought — used against Jogendra Chandra Bose and Tilak.
4. The 1910 Act revived the worst features of the VPA 1878.

**However, the nationalists stayed determined**
1. Changed over to English to avoid the VPA ⟹ *Amrit Bazar Patrika*.
2. Agitations for liberty — e.g. role of the Indian National Association in the VPA repeal.
3. Use of the press as a source of <u>public empowerment</u>.

All this still holds true, as the recent repeal of <u>sedition</u> in the new criminal laws has been done for press freedom.
