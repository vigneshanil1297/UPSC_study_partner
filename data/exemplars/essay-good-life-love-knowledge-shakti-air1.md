# Topic: A good life is one inspired by love and guided by knowledge

What are humans if not just flesh and blood? How do they differ from other creatures? What differentiates an animal existence from human existence? All these questions have just one single answer — actions guided by knowledge and inspired by emotions. Every creature on Earth comes to live its own life span, but what is unique with humans is that they can create an everlasting impact on others as well. Man is a *situated self*; he does not live in isolation, rather he interacts with his environment, creating huge impacts and in turn getting impacted as well.

But what is a life — or, to be more specific, what is a *good* life? Was it not the luxurious life that Buddha lived in the palace, which had all the facilities to ensure a smooth life with no hardship? If this was a good life, why did he leave his comfort at the young age of 29? Because Buddha was a man of values; he realised that the real meaning of a good life does not revolve around materialistic pleasure, rather it is about the eternal knowledge that guides the whole world, coupled with the spirit of love and compassion for fellow beings. Thus, after attaining Nirvana, Buddha did not stop — he went on to preach his philosophy of love and brotherhood, i.e. Dhamma, so as to benefit mankind. The best manifestation of Buddha's selfless good living — where he not just gained but preached knowledge — was Ashoka. Ashoka, one of the most prominent rulers of the Mauryan empire, was guided by a policy of war and conquest just like any other ruler; but once he came across the philosophy of Buddha he understood the importance of peace and brotherhood, and hence Ashoka's ideology changed from Bherighosha (life of war) to Dhammaghosha (life of Dharma).

Thus the idea of a good life is more than just knowledge and skills; it is rather about the right attitude and the right moral aptitude, and it must be understood that love alone can inspire us only to a limit — it will always require the backup of knowledge. India has had such great leaders who have set examples of what a good life looks like. Dr APJ Abdul Kalam, also known as the missile man of India, lived a balanced representation of knowledge and love for the country. Despite being offered to work with NASA, Kalam chose to work for ISRO and help India realise its true potential; his decision was based on his love and dedication towards India and his knowledge, and can be called a "man of high integrity." It is easy to live a life of ignorance for those who look at the world through tainted glasses, because a life of ignorance is free from responsibilities and accountability. But as Socrates says, "An unexamined life is not worth living." Thus humans must continuously strive to achieve the true purpose of their living. Another example in this regard is Mother Teresa, who lived a selfless life serving others and giving back the knowledge of compassion, empathy and love to the world.

Being ignorant can help us move past a particular event, but how does that serve the purpose of being human? Imagine if our freedom fighters like Ambedkar and Gandhi — who were educated and had degrees in law — and Bose, who qualified the civil services examination, would have focused on ensuring a good life for themselves and would have ignored the plight of the masses. Where would have India been today? It was their vision of a good life that created a good life for us. It was their selfless action — inspired by love and guided by knowledge — that led millions towards a life of dignity by restoring Swaraj for them.

Today, in the contemporary era, the advent of technologies like information-communication, Artificial Intelligence and robotics has made life easier for humans — but is it contributing to a good living? Partially yes, as they have their set of advantages; but partially no, because they have disastrous negative outcomes as well. AI has made things very easy for humans, but has several ethical concerns: it has led to an increase in plagiarism, violation of privacy, deepfake pornography, etc. Humans need to understand that technologies are a double-edged sword and hence must be handled with caution. It requires humans to be virtuous, such that their actions are guided by greater good and not personal pleasure and satisfaction — thus in contemporary times the idea of guided human action becomes all the more important.

Another aspect in this regard is the environment, which has been exploited by the human race for its own benefit for centuries, leading to a disturbance in ecological balance that is evident in the form of climate change. Even then the international community has not been able to reach a consensus on how to manage climate change; developed as well as developing countries are involved in a strife over blame-game, because their idea of a good life is guided by hedonistic principles and not inclusive and sustainable growth. Thus there is a need for countries to understand that "Earth provides enough for everyone's need, but not everyone's greed" — M.K. Gandhi. Their decision and action must be inspired by love for nature and guided by knowledge of sustainability.

The reality is, today this 8 billion people will live and go and yet again many more will come, but there are just a few who will make the difference and speak up for the greater good — whose action will be guided by the idea of *Nishkama Karma*, like Medha Patkar, Malala, Greta Thunberg, etc. This is the time we need to wake up from the slumber and decide the right course of action, as recently the UNGA chief held that "the era of global warming has ended and the era of global boiling has started." Thus there is a need for collaboration among the international community to ensure a good life. The human consciousness is what makes them unique, and this is what they must remember and use as a tool to ensure that this planet becomes a better place to live. Only focusing on creating a good life for oneself will not yield that satisfaction which contributing towards a good life for others will. It just takes a little extra feeling of compassion and empathy for others, along with the right knowledge. Thus one needs to move from a quantitative description of living towards a qualitative enrichment.
