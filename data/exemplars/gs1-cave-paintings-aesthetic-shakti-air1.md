Paintings are a very crucial source of history, which depict the way of <u>life</u>, <u>culture</u>, <u>language</u>, etc. of any given period and hence are used extensively by historians to study the past.

**Significance of Cave Paintings**

Paintings have been used since the prehistoric times in the form of cave paintings:—

1. **Stone Age** — Paintings at Sohagighat (UP) and Lakhudiyar (Uttarakhand) caves depict stick-line animal and human figures showing scenes of <u>hunting</u> and <u>social gathering</u>.
2. **Indus Valley Civilisation** — The paintings were made on walls for aesthetic purpose.
3. **Ancient India** — This era is home to the origin of several caves and their paintings.

   [Linkage flowchart: Ajanta caves → Bagh caves → Ellora caves]
   - **Ajanta caves** — have paintings of Bodhisattvas as well as kings and their palaces.
   - **Bagh caves** — in MP, have paintings of Lord Vishnu and his boar (Varaha) incarnation.
   - **Ellora caves** — Cave 16, known as the Kailasa temple, has a painting of Ravana shaking Mount Kailash.
   - **Udayagiri–Khandgiri caves** — in Orissa, by king Kharavela; have the Jain Namokar mantra.

Thus the cave paintings had:—
1. Aesthetic significance, i.e. for decoration.
2. Religious significance.
3. Depiction of the way of life.

…and have been prominent in every age.
