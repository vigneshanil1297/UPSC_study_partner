# Topic: Discuss the feminist approach to security.

Feminism is a theory that aims at addressing women's exploitation, ending patriarchy and aiming for equality of all. It is an *emancipatory* theory in international politics.

Security in the traditional sense is seen as nation-centric, aiming to protect territorial boundaries and sovereignty (*Morgenthau*). But feminist scholars criticise this view as *male-centric* and *one-dimensional*. *J. N. Tickner* says security is multidimensional — the focus should shift from the nation's boundaries to *human security*, which essentially encompasses women's security.

In contemporary times, wars and conflicts are threatening women's food and bodily security. COVID has exacerbated inequalities, and mainstream theories remain male-centric. In these times, states should ensure women's security from both external and internal exploitation — e.g. Iran's crackdown on the hijab.

The *Critical school* also supports the feminist view, as they too aim for a 'human-centric' view of security.
