# Topic: Critically analyse Hannah Arendt's view on ideology.

Hannah Arendt was a Jew who lived during the time of Hitler. She has extensively analysed totalitarianism in her book *The Origins of Totalitarianism*.

She explains that totalitarian leaders rely on *ideology* and *violence* to propagate their rule; thus ideology is an instrument of totalitarianism. She says ideology defines the limits of the common sense of people and turns people into masses — ideology is an instrument of mobilising masses. For instance, nationalism as used by fascist leaders to unify people, diluting all differences, leads to 'mass politics'. It is effective when there is a critical mass who want to escape reality. This idea is similar to Marx, who says ideology is "false consciousness" and acts as the "opium of the masses."

But her ideas are criticised as one-sided, as ideology can also unify people towards progress — e.g. the Hindu way of life. Hannah's idea is important to understand the misuse of ideas to fool the masses, and to prevent it.
