# Topic: How does the debate around the Rawlsian notion of justice make justice a pluralistic concept?

Justice is an *architectonic concept* and the most foundational value in political science. Thinkers from the time of Plato have deliberated upon justice, producing diverse notions. Rawls is credited for the revival of political science and normative theory with his *Theory of Justice*.

In his theory, he uses the hypothetical device of the *veil of ignorance* and the *original position* to arrive at principles of justice without any bias. People are gathered to decide the principles of distribution of public primary goods. They decide:
i) Maximum equal liberty for all,
ii) Equality of opportunity,
iii) Maximum advantage to the least advantaged (difference principle).
These principles are in lexical order.

**How it led to debates and made justice pluralistic —**

These principles of justice have been criticised by *Communitarians*, as they focus on the role of community in man's life. Michael Sandel says man is an embedded / situated self, whereas Rawls's man is atomistic. Michael Walzer says "justice is not the science of homogenisation but the art of differentiation" — meaning principles must vary, as there are differences within and between societies.

Similarly, a *feminist* view of justice came up, criticising the Rawlsian contract as masculinist (Carole Pateman's *Sexual Contract*): gender should be accounted for in these principles.

*Marxist* scholars have their own notion of justice as giving everyone a share in resources, ensuring equality of outcome. While *Dworkin* argues for ensuring equality of resources, *Amartya Sen* takes a step forward and focuses on equality of capabilities.

*Neoliberals* like *Nozick* criticise Rawls and gave the *Entitlement theory* of justice, which focuses on 'merit' and 'liberty'.

Thus, these debates surrounding Rawls's idea enriched justice as a *pluralistic idea*, which helped in ensuring a welfare state and a just society. Rawls's theory became the starting point of justice and its journey.
