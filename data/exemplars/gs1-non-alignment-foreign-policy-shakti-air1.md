# Topic: Do you agree that non-alignment adopted after independence formed the bed-rock of Indian Foreign policy? Substantiate.

Foreign policy of a country can be defined as the sum total of its <u>principles</u>, <u>interests</u> and <u>objectives</u> that it seeks to achieve through its relation with another country.

**Non-Aligned Movement & India's Foreign policy**

1. NAM was the brainchild of our then PM — <u>Jawaharlal Nehru</u> — and was supported by leaders of other nations: [Egypt — Abdel Nasser; Indonesia — Sukarno; Yugoslavia — J.B. Tito; Ghana — Kwame Nkrumah].
2. It was brought to save the decolonised nations from becoming a <u>chessboard of Cold War politics</u>.
3. NAM for India stood for no friendship, no animosity — rather the focus was on having a <u>principled stand</u> in international politics.
4. NAM still today forms the <u>bedrock of Indian foreign policy</u>.
5. In this multipolar world, India is focusing its decisions based on <u>national interest</u>.
6. e.g. During the Russia–Ukraine war, India refused to take sides but continued its diplomatic relation with <u>Russia</u> (purchasing oil).
7. Even with respect to trade (e.g. the <u>MSP issue in the WTO</u>), India does not silently obey the demand of capitalist nations, nor totally rely on socialistic principles; instead, our international trade is also built on a <u>Mixed economy</u> model.

Thus even today, after 75 years of independence, NAM continues to guide India's foreign policy.
