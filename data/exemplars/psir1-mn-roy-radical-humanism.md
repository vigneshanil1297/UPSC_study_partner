# Topic: M.N. Roy conceived of humanism as scientific, integral and a radically new worldview. Comment.

M.N. Roy was a Marxist scholar whose ideational journey led him to his theory of *Radical Humanism*, differing from any other ideas.

For Roy, humanism was focused on 'man as a rational being'. Man is capable of controlling nature by the virtue of scientific knowledge — hence humanism is *scientific*. Similarly, humanism is based on the values of "cosmopolitan unity of free beings," as M.N. Roy said; this means his idea of humanism focused on breaking barriers of nation, religion, etc., and called for human unity — hence it is *integral*. In this spirit he criticised religion and nationalism as chains which limit the human mind; man should focus on scientific progress instead.

His ideas were *radical* as they focused on man as a rational being — his most original self — and on radical change in society for the progress of humanity. His ideas are relevant today, as the world is facing a crisis of humanity — wars, COVID — making his call for a scientific, integral and human-centred worldview a genuinely new alternative to both liberal individualism and orthodox Marxism.
